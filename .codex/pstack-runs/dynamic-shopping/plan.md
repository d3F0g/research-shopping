## Falsified premise

A fixed set of category-specific page grammars can reliably normalize arbitrary live web products into useful recommendations. The genuine checkpoint contradicts this premise: relevant laptop pages and native observations are retrieved, yet every identity is rejected; no published price survives any of the three requests; the excluded-brand commuting request yields only an unverified identity. A source quote's literal presence is necessary but not sufficient to establish its product, variant, condition or eligibility.

## Scope and verification predicate

Normal development accepts open product types and request-specific requirements. No static category or feature enum may gate normal research or contextual editing. Preserve exact source-backed identity, variants, prices and citations; unknown or contradictory hard requirements cannot authorize a confident recommendation. Offer a justified conditional recommendation where the observed evidence supports one, and state meaningful unknowns. Do not invent measured performance, current price, stock or merchant offers.

Use the three actual checkpoint requests and at least one materially different product class. Success requires relevant distinct alternatives, useful cited comparison, qualified observed pricing when the sources supply it, correct constraints and a reasoned choice. Safe returned URLs and independently checked product destinations complete the journey. Do not claim all products or arbitrary-language understanding from four examples. Automated replay remains explicitly labeled test evidence; genuine proof uses the unchanged normal app composition.
